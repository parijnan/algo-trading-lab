**Name:** Janus (user, 2026-10-05): the two-faced god of thresholds, for levels read two ways (fade or break). Roman where the other engines are Greek, a deliberate nod to this being a different track. A working name, not necessarily the name of any engine that comes out of it.

## 1. The question

Do the Camarilla levels computed from the previous session's high, low and close carry any tradable intraday information on MCX futures, after realistic costs, and if so in which instruments and which regimes? Two classic readings of the same levels:

- **Mean reversion:** price reaching R3/S3 tends to fall back toward the pivot zone, so fade the touch with a stop beyond R4/S4.
- **Breakout:** price closing beyond R4/S4 tends to continue to R5/S5, so trade the break.

The levels are the same, the trades are opposite. Which one (if either) is right is a property of the regime and instrument, so the first study is descriptive, before any strategy is tuned.

## 2. Levels (all parameters live in `janus_backtest/configs.py`, nothing hardcoded)

For previous session high H, low L, close C, range R = H - L:

- R1..R4 = C + R x (1.1/12, 1.1/6, 1.1/4, 1.1/2); S1..S4 = C - R x the same factors.
- R5 = (H / L) x C and S5 = C - (R5 - C) (the common extension); R6 = R5 + 1.168 x (R5 - R4) and S6 mirrored about C (the usual second breakout target; computed from 2026-10-05, user's call: it costs nothing).
- R7 = R6 + 1.168 x (R6 - R5) and S7 mirrored about C (added 2026-10-06 as the third breakout target, user's call). There is no standard definition of a seventh level: this continues the same geometric ladder and is a convention, `R7_FACTOR` in configs.
- Pivot (the usual central level) = (H + L + C) / 3, kept as an optional reference, not part of the core levels.

Choices to settle in configs and test one at a time: the session close C is the last 1-minute close of the previous session (MCX settlement prices differ slightly and are not in our data, so this is a stated approximation); session = one calendar trading day, 09:00 to 23:30 (23:55 in US DST periods), which already ends before midnight so there is no overnight-session ambiguity; the factor 1.1 is the standard value and the grid is fixed unless a later experiment varies it.

## 4. Phases

**Phase 0, descriptive, no strategy.** For each instrument and each session compute the levels, then measure: how often price touches each of S3/R3/S4/R4/S5/R5; what happens after a touch (reverts to the central zone, ends the session beyond the level, how far it travels in each direction before the session close, forward excursions in R multiples); and how all of that depends on where the session opens (inside the H3/L3 band, between H3 and H4, beyond H4, gap size in R). Output: tables and a short report. This decides whether a strategy phase is warranted and which reading (reversion or breakout) the data supports. No parameters are optimised here.

**Phase 1, rule-based strategies on what Phase 0 supports.** One variable changed per experiment (repo convention). Candidate rules, to be pinned in configs before running: reversion entry at the first touch of R3/S3 (limit at the level, or on the first bar closing back inside), stop at R4/S4 or a fraction of R, target at the central zone or a fixed multiple of R; breakout entry on a 15-minute close beyond R4/S4, stop back inside H3/L3, target R5/S5 or a trailing exit; one trade per direction per session, flat at the session close, no entries in the first N minutes and none near the close (values in configs). Costs and slippage per instrument in configs (tick-based), applied from the first run, never added after.

**Phase 2, robustness.** Walk-forward by year (2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 year-to-date), per-instrument and pooled, parameter sensitivity around the chosen config, regime splits (volatility terciles, trend vs range days by the prior day's behaviour, month-end and expiry weeks), a shuffled-levels null (the same rules on levels computed from a random other day) to show the specific Camarilla geometry adds something beyond "any support and resistance". The Prometheus work showed an edge can be regime-dependent and vanish in earlier data, so out-of-sample years are the main test, not the pooled P&L.

**Phase 3, decision.** Only if Phase 2 holds: a go/no-go note with the instrument list, rules and expected frequency; building an engine would be a separate plan (the Hestia engine interface), not part of this research.

## 5. Evaluation metrics

Trades, win rate, average win and loss in R and in Rs per lot, total P&L, maximum drawdown, Calmar (unitless and annualised on the same basis used in `prometheus_backtest/README.md` so numbers are comparable), longest losing streak, trades per month, share of P&L from the top 5 trades (concentration), and results by year. Slippage and costs always on.

## 8. Phase 0 result (2026-10-05)

Built and run on the four instruments with the defaults above (session close = last 1-minute close; R5/S5 and the central reference included; all Fyers history from each instrument's start, void skipped; re-run 2026-10-07 with the void filled and included, 884 to 1,383 sessions, no conclusion changed, every excess moved by under 3 points). On every instrument, after a touch of R3/S3 the inward level (R2/S2) came first less often than the driftless benchmark (75% for the R4-versus-R2 pair): excess between -6 and -11 percentage points, same sign in nearly every year and largest in 2026; after R4/S4 the move on to R5/S5 beat the benchmark by a similar margin. So the levels behave more like continuation than reversion zones in this data. It is not an edge yet: costs are not applied, and touching an extreme level selects trending days, so any "extreme day keeps going" effect would show the same sign. Phase 2's shuffled-levels null is the test that separates the two. Next step if the user agrees: Phase 1 breakout rules (the reading Phase 0 leans toward), with the reversion rules run alongside as the control.

### 9a. Feasibility of "equal thirds, trail on T2, re-entry of the booked lot" (user idea 2026-10-11; descriptive pass run the same day)

The idea as first stated: equal thirds at T1, T2 and T3; after T2 the stop trails to cost or to T1; because the stop stays at its original level until T2, a pullback after T1 does not stop the position, which leaves a chance to re-buy the booked lot at a lower price for the same targets. Code `janus_backtest/janus_setups.py` and `thirds_feasibility.py`, tests `tests/test_janus_setups.py` (20, each new behaviour mutation-checked), output `janus_backtest/outputs/thirds_feasibility_summary.txt`. Descriptive only: the first touch entry of each setup per session (fade = reclaim of S3/R3, breakout = break of R4/S4, both directions), setups measured independently (no daily cap, no stop-and-reverse chain), original stop kept until T2, no costs, no entry in the session's first minute, a bar that reaches both the stop and a target counts as the stop, 8,309 entries over 4 instruments (2021 to Oct 2026).

**Geometry correction (the earlier chat table was wrong):** R5 = C x H/L, so the breakout ladder is much wider than the R3-to-R4 spacing suggested. Reward in units of the stop distance (R3 to R4 or S3 to S4): breakout T1/T2/T3 = 1.7 / 3.6 / 5.9; fade T1/T2/T3 = 1.33 / 1.67 / 2.0 (the fade targets R1, R2, R3 are crowded, 0.09 of the previous range apart). The stop distance is a small fraction of price: median 0.2% (GOLDPETAL), 0.4-0.5% (SILVERMIC), 0.6-0.7% (CRUDEOILM), 1.1-1.2% (NATGASMINI), so costs are a large part of one stop on GOLDPETAL.

**Results (pooled, fade / breakout):** T1 before the stop 38% / 34% (stop first 46% / 47%, the rest unresolved at the close), against driftless 43% / 38% on the decided cases, so a small excess before costs. After T1, price comes back to the entry price before T2 in 20% / 36% of cases (the re-entry opportunity, 386 / 409 events, about 4 a month per instrument across both families). A lot re-bought at the entry price and aimed at T1 again wins 53% / 51% of decided cases against a driftless 55% / 48%, expectancy -0.1 / +0.1 stop distances gross, standard error about 0.07, and the sign flips between instruments and years: no demonstrated edge, and costs would turn it negative. After T2 with the stop trailed: to cost it is hit before T3 in 17% / 12%, to T1 in 58% / 29% (fixed stop reaches T3 in 80% / 38%). Trailing to T1 is clearly destructive in the fade family because T1, T2 and T3 are only 0.33 stop distances apart.

**Not yet done:** the P&L comparison of thirds with a fixed stop, a cost trail, a T1 trail, and re-entry (needs the user to confirm the re-entry rules: which level, which lots, which target and stop for the re-bought lot, whether it counts against the 3-entry cap), costs from the first run, and the re-bought lot aimed at T2 or T3. The cost trail and re-entry at the entry price are the same event: once the runner's stop is at cost, a pullback to the entry price stops it out and re-buys the booked lots at the same price on the same touch.

### 9b. Day simulation of the decided rules (run 2026-10-11)

Code `janus_backtest/janus_engine.py` (event-by-event walk along each bar's price path; four setups side by side; stop-and-reverse falls out of shared levels), runner `thirds_pnl.py`, tests `tests/test_janus_engine.py` (21, every behaviour mutation-checked; one tie-order mutant is behaviourally equivalent), outputs `outputs/thirds_pnl_summary.txt` and `outputs/thirds_parity.txt`. Rules: equal thirds, original stop, no trail, no cap on entries; arms one change at a time: A0 thirds only, A1 plus re-buy of booked lots at the entry level, A2 plus counter trade at a fade's T3, A3 both (the user's design). Each arm under two bar-order assumptions (neutral; adverse = against the open net position). Touch entries and stops, touch fills for limit-type orders, A3x = A3 with one tick required through each limit level. PROVISIONAL and not yet decided: no entry in the first minute, no entry/re-buy/counter in the last 60 minutes, hard exit 15 minutes before the last bar, costs swept at 0 / 0.02 / 0.05 / 0.10% of price per leg (entry plus exit of one lot). **Results (pooled over four instruments, neutral / adverse bar order):** A0 gross 1,527% / 1,365% with break-even cost 0.05% / 0.04% per leg; A1 +1% / +2% gross on 4% more legs, break-even 0.04%; A2 -14% / -17% gross; A3 -14% / -16% gross against A0. ### 9c. Entry redesign: close-armed watcher with a limit order at the level (user design 2026-10-11; run the same day)

Entry rule (all four setups, mirrored for shorts): the watcher is IDLE until a bar CLOSES on the arming side of the entry level (fade long: below S3), which arms it; the next close on the entry side (above S3) is the signal; then a limit order sits at exactly the level and fills only if price trades back to it (a retest). If price reaches T1 first the order is cancelled and the watcher goes back to IDLE; a finished trade also resets it. Close timeframe was not specified, so it is a grid: 1, 5 and 15 minutes (clock-aligned buckets of the 1-minute data). `ENTRY_SCENARIOS` / `RUN_PLAN` in `janus_configs.py`, engine mode `close_limit`, tests in `tests/test_janus_engine.py` (34, each new behaviour mutation-checked; two survivors are equivalent defence in depth), `thirds_pnl.py` (simulate one symbol, then `--report`), output `outputs/thirds_pnl_summary.txt`. The touch design of 9b is kept as scenario `touch` for comparison. Stops, targets, re-buys and the counter trade at T3 are unchanged; everything else provisional as in 9b.

**Results (pooled over four instruments, gross percent of price per lot summed over legs; A0 = plain thirds):** touch 1,527; c1 556; c5 699; c15 630. Net at 0.02% per leg: touch +851, c1 -14, c5 +238, c15 +269; at 0.05% every realistic scenario is negative (c15 -273, c5 -455). Signals filled: 90% (c1), 84% (c5), 78% (c15), the rest cancelled at T1 (689 / 966 / 1,207 signals) or never retested. By family (c15): breakouts +448 gross (break-even 0.06% per leg, +70 net at 0.05%), fades +182 (break-even 0.02%, negative net at 0.02%). By instrument (c15): CRUDEOILM +203, NATGASMINI +210, SILVERMIC +182, GOLDPETAL +36. Re-buy (A1) adds +9 to +15 gross against A0, counter trade (A2) subtracts 21 to 30, A3 is below A0 at every timeframe. One tick through each limit level (A0x) costs 7 to 12% of gross. Bar-order assumption barely matters here.

**Why the realistic limit entry gives up so much:** a limit at the level fills mainly when the reclaim or break fails (adverse selection) and misses the moves that run away. The LITERAL reading (scenarios l1/l5/l15: bought at the level on the signal close although price is already above it) shows 2,369 / 3,400 / 4,188 gross but is NOT achievable: it hands the whole distance between the level and the signal close to every trade for free. Reference only.

**Open:** real round-trip cost per lot (item 10) decides whether anything here is tradable; a marketable entry at the next bar's open after the confirming close (what can actually be done) has not been run; breakout-only book; cap; stop trigger grid.

**Reading of the touch-versus-close comparison (discussed 2026-10-12):** touch is ahead, but it only loses less, since neither is profitable at 0.05% per leg (touch -164, c15 -273). The comparison is not like for like: touch takes many more trades, so part of its gross lead is volume, and its fills are generous (breakout touch entries are stop orders and would slip; slippage not modelled). The weakness of the new design is the limit order at the level (adverse selection), not necessarily the close confirmation itself, which is untested in a fair form. **Parked 2026-10-12 by the user ("we'll come back to this later"). Next when resumed, in this order:** (1) real round-trip cost per lot (item 10); (2) marketable entry at the next bar's open after the confirming close, for 1/5/15-minute closes, with a breakout-only book included (fades break even at only ~0.02% per leg in every design); (3) direct cap test; (4) stop-trigger grid; (5) counter trade that waits for a reclaim.

**Range-based levels (user design 2026-10-12, separate plan `plans/janus-range-levels.md`):** High and Low for the Camarilla formulas come from a range tracked on daily bars with setter-candle logic (close strictly outside the range starts a new range, otherwise wick extension, no confirmation, no minimum length), Close stays the previous session's close, range derived per traded contract. Designed, not built.
