## 2. Reconnaissance: what the recorded days can and cannot prove

The logs record every 15-minute bar as `15m bar HH:MM — ST=… close=…  no flip | FLIP -> direction`, plus every order, fill, entry, exit and Rule 7 line, so the logs are a complete decision oracle. Comparing Hestia's data path (`history.resample_1m` and `indicators.compute_st` over the pipeline's 1-minute file, seeded 18 days back) with the logged bars, per day:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 09-15 | 54 | 30 | 3 | 0 | 24 (0.19) |
| 09-16 | 57 | 21 | 5 | 0 | 35 (3.25) |
| 09-17 | 57 | 41 | 4 | 0 | 12 (0.13) |
| 09-18 | 57 | 36 | 5 | 0 | 19 (0.40) |
| 09-21 | 57 | 24 | 5 | 0 | 31 (1.93) |

| total | 510 | 308 | 45 | **2** | 179 |

**Findings.**
1. **The implementation is right.** 09-24 matches to the logged precision (ST within 0.005, every flip), and 508 of 510 flip verdicts agree over all ten days. The seed, the resampler, the 09:00 anchor and Supertrend are the same numbers as live.
2. **The differences are the live system's own imperfections, not Hestia's.** A live bar is built once at its boundary from whatever minutes the poller has delivered (waiting up to the cutoff), and is never rebuilt when a late minute arrives; the pipeline's nightly file is the after-the-fact complete one. Example, 09-16 13:00: live close 9526, pipeline 9531, because the live window lacked its last minute. One missing minute shifts a close by a point or two, and because Supertrend's ATR carries history, the ST then differs by tenths for hours. Hestia's live data service builds bars the same way, so it will have the same behaviour live; nothing is to be "fixed" here.
3. **The two flip mismatches are one razor-thin cross.** 09-23 22:15: live close 8791 against a live ST of 8791.89 (bearish, entry at 22:30, trade 38); the pipeline's slightly different ST is 8790.63, so its close of 8791 does not cross and the flip appears one bar later. That is exactly the thin-cross class the provisional margin guard is about, caused by an earlier partial bar the same day (21:15 live ST 8778.44 vs 8780.54).
4. **The recorded window contains no contract roll.** The roll to the 2026-10-19 contract happened around 09-15/16, and the log window has no rollover or coincident-flip decisions. It does contain: Rule 7 flips (many), target1 and target2 scale-outs, one stop-loss (trade 43, 09-24 23:15), Kill Switch events on 09-21 and 09-22, and a missed flip recovered at the 09-23 start. The first live roll of this contract is around 2026-10-12 to 10-13 (the 5-trading-day window before the 2026-10-19 expiry, holiday-aware; roll eve is the evening before). **So the roll logic cannot be verified by replay; it needs the unit tests of Prometheus's own rollover suite ported, the historical parity tests, and a DRY_RUN Selene roll (P8) as the first real exercise.** The user should know this before P6.

**Consequence for the method.** Two tiers, both needed:
- **Tier 1, decision replay.** Drive the engine with the *logged* bar events (bar time, close, ST, flip; the previous bar's ST is the previous log line's) and the 1-minute prices for the LTP path, and compare its requests and state changes with the logged orders and `prometheus_trades.csv`. This isolates the engine's decisions from data imperfections and can match exactly on flips and entries; exits by stop or target are compared to the minute (live watched ticks every half second, replay has 1-minute highs and lows).
- **Tier 2, data-path agreement** (the table above): kept as a permanent regression check against the pulled files, with the accepted tolerance stated (flip verdicts within 2 of 510, ST exact on a clean day).

## 5. P5.1 record (2026-09-28): `hestia_core/roll_policy.py`

Done. A pure module (no I/O, no clock, no broker, no engine state; a test asserts its imports) that holds the roll rules for every engine, ported from `prometheus_production/prometheus.py` sections 4 to 9 and 18: `effective_contract` (the front, or the next out at 5 trading days left counting today, holiday-aware, with `no_next` when nothing later is listed), `roll_needed_tonight` (tomorrow's *trading* day), `arm_action` (flat: switch now, in a trade: arm the evening), `flat_switch_due` (a plain watching state and no Rule 7 flip in flight), `rollover_time` (23:15, or 23:40 on a 23:55 close), `entry_suppressed`, `basis_price` (the new contract's nearest close within 5 minutes of the old entry, the earliest on a tie, never a guess), `reopen_plan` (sized to the surviving lots, lot2-only when only the far-target lot survived), `decide_rollover` (flatten unconditionally; reopen only when the new contract's supertrend agrees, the optional alignment filter agrees and a basis exists; unavailable data is a no-go), `coincident_flip` (same direction on the same bar with a full 15-row window) and `restart_action` (none, catch up to a later contract, missed roll, or refuse to roll backwards).

## 7. P5.3 record (2026-09-28): `prometheus_engine/`

Done, not yet replayed against the recorded days (that is P5.4). `prometheus_engine/engine.py` is the decision layer of `prometheus.py` written against `EngineContext`; `engine_configs.py` holds every parameter (pinned to `prometheus_production/prometheus_configs.py` by a test while both exist); `levels.py` holds the pure stop, target and P&L arithmetic (pinned to production's own `resolve_thresholds` and `resolve_target2` source by AST extraction); `state.py` is the engine's persisted state (JSON, one blob through `ctx.save_state`). The factory is `prometheus_engine.engine:build`, the one `hestia_config.ENGINES` already names; the entry stays `enabled=False` until cutover (P6).

**How it works.** One `next_event` loop: `SessionStart` (choose the contract, reconcile with the ledger, settle any request that was pending at the last stop, arm the roll, catch a missed flip), `BarComplete` and `ProvisionalBar` (the signal), `RequestOutcome` (the only place position state changes), `CommandEvent(EXIT)`, and a tick between events for the LTP-driven stop and targets. The engine sends **one request per decision** and persists its id and purpose (`state.pending`) before sending; while any request is outstanding it makes no decision that could overlap it (a flip bar that arrives then is dropped with a warning, a tick that finds a pending flip waits). Ids are `prometheus-<date>-t<trade number at decision time>-<purpose>-<attempt>` (a roll reopen therefore carries the closing trade's number; uniqueness comes from the purpose and attempt), so a re-send after a failure carries a new attempt number and a restart never reuses a settled one. Stop = `FlattenRequest`; a target = `CloseRequest` of that lot's lots; Rule 7 = one `FlipRequest` (close the old lots and open the new ones as one netted order); the operator's EXIT = `FlattenRequest(MANUAL_EXIT)` re-sent every tick until flat; the stop is checked before the targets, so it wins a same-tick tie.

**Deliberate differences from the standalone process.**
- An entry that fills only partly is accepted as it is; the standalone process kept retrying the remainder of a flip's re-entry indefinitely. Hestia never tops an entry up.
- A failed exit or flip is re-sent after `retry_cooldown_s` under a new request id (Hestia has already retried inside the request); a REJECTED outcome first reconciles the engine's belief with the ledger, which wins.
- **Both remaining items in this line were reproduced, 2026-09-29, on the same day the live gap mattered.** The periodic Slack P&L update (`_maybe_send_trade_update`/`_compute_trade_pnl`, every `TRADE_UPDATE_SEC`/20s to `#trade-updates`, Slack-only, never logged) and the per-minute running-row trade log (`_maybe_send_running_row`/`_running_row`, every `running_row_sec`/60s while in-trade plus one row per lot at the moment it exits with `exit_reason` set, written one file per trade by a new host-owned `RunningRowWriter`/`ctx.report_running_row` — mirroring `report_trade`'s own "engine reports, host persists" split, under `hestia_data/trades/running_rows/<engine>/`). Both omissions above were mistakes caught live by the user on the first live day, not a considered design decision ("I don't want features that were already tested being skipped... there's a reason for the 20 second updates and that's to have proper visibility into trade performance"); corrected the same day. One remaining, deliberate gap: no `[PAPER]` prefix on the periodic Slack message (production's own convention for a DRY_RUN trade) — `EngineContext` has no way for an engine to ask whether it is the paper or live instance, and adding that is a small interface change not yet made; low priority since the message content itself (direction, entry, LTP, P&L) is otherwise complete and correct for both.
- Roll rules are `roll_policy`'s: a missing next contract flattens at the rollover time (`flatten_done` then keeps the engine out for the rest of the night). One addition found in review: the roll is armed only when tomorrow's effective contract expires **after** the one being traded, so a catch-up onto a later contract never rolls backwards.
- Dynamic sizing is capped at the unit cap (Hestia would refuse more anyway).
- **Failure paths around a roll (found by the advisor review, fixed and tested).** A rejected roll close arrives with its dependent reopen as DEPENDENCY_FAILED; the engine takes no structural action on a reopen outcome while the old position is still open, so it never moves to the new contract with the old position open. The fallback roll re-runs from the armed `roll_target` on the tick under fresh ids; a missed roll (restart onto a contract already inside the window) is now armed as an `immediate` roll so the same tick retries it, and an immediate roll owns the transition (a flip bar in that window is ignored with a warning). A rejected coincident-flip close is retried at once as an exit-only flip on the old contract (`pending_flip` with no new lots); the coincident re-entry on the new contract is given up in that case, deliberately.

**Verification.** `tests/test_prometheus_engine.py` (38) and `tests/test_prometheus_engine_roll.py` (14), on the fake Hestia over scripted price paths so flips land where the test needs them. Parity: config against production's, levels and P&L against production's own function source. Trade rows carry exactly the shared columns (empty for a lot never opened) and production's exit-reason strings (`stop_loss`, `target1`, `target2_flat_pct`, `trend_flip`, `slack_exit`, `rollover`, checked against the pulled trades file); P&L is points x lots x lot size. Scenarios: entry, both targets and re-arming, levels off the real fill, Rule 7 as one 4-lot order with the old trade booked at the flip's fill, the stop, a stale price never triggering a stop, a partial entry, a failed entry, a failed exit re-sent until it lands, an UNCONFIRMED exit changing nothing, EXIT (with and without a position), margin refusal, dynamic sizing and its cap, provisional flips (confirmed, disagreeing, margin guard, disabled), an engine crash and resume without a second entry, the ledger-wins cases both ways, the entry and exit timing guards, roll flows (flat-on-eve switch, in-trade arming, fallback carry with the basis recalibration, veto, no next contract, coincident flip, exit-only flip, missed roll in both directions, catch-up, and a rejected fallback close, missed-roll close and coincident-flip close each retried). Whole suite 557 passed, 2 skipped (the two known Iris skips). **Mutation checks** (a scratch script, ten mutations, nine killed): the state replaced instead of reset in place (found for real during the build: a Rule 7 flip re-fired 3,670 times because `_finalize_trade` swapped the state object under a caller holding the old one), the stop comparison inverted, stale prices accepted, ledger reconciliation removed, the flip-overlap guard removed, the provisional margin measured against the current instead of the previous supertrend, the reopen outcome switching contracts with the old position open, the coincident-close retry marker removed, and the missed-roll retry removed were all killed. The tenth (a flip closing lot 1 before lot 2) survives and is benign, because Hestia completes exits so both lots always close at one price.

**Interface gaps the engine works around (for the plan's open list).** `latest_bar` on a tracked contract carries no minute count, so the coincident-flip completeness check (`rows_in_window`) is assumed complete; a tracked contract's completeness is undocumented; there are no per-minute running-row trade files. ## 8. P5.4 record (2026-09-28): Tier 1 replay and the injected faults

Done for the days the price data covers. Tool: `python -m prometheus_engine.replay_check` (module `prometheus_engine/replay_check.py`). It drives the engine on the fake Hestia with the bars and Supertrend values live Prometheus logged (`recorded.LoggedReplayData`) and the pipeline's 1-minute prices, seeds the position live carried into the day from the trades file, and compares every entry (time, direction, units) and every lot exit (lot, reason, time) with the log. Each day is replayed on its own, so a difference never cascades; a separate chained run carries the engine's own saved state and the ledger across nights.

**Result: 88 of 88 live decisions reproduced with nothing extra, over nine sessions (2026-09-16, 17, 18, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 28).** (Updated 2026-09-29 after the second Delos pull and datasync brought 09-28's trades and price data in: 17 of 17 for that day alone, including the trade-51 stop-loss.) That window contains Rule 7 flips (many), target 1 and target 2 scale-outs (including the 09:01 target 1 that filled on the weekend gap on 09-21), one stop loss on both lots (trade 43, 09-25 09:01, a gap through the stop; a second, trade 51 on 09-28 21:50), two Kill Switch stops with a restart minutes later and a position open (09-21, 09-22), and a missed flip recovered at start (09-23: the 23:15 bar the previous session ended before reaching, exit only because it is inside the entry guard). Bar-driven decisions match to the second; price-driven ones (targets, stop) match within a minute (tolerance 150 s) because live watched ticks and replay has the 1-minute file; fill prices differ by 0.4 to 2.5 points on average and 10 at most (the 09:00 start-up exit on 09-23 against the 1-minute price). Ten sessions were pulled; 09-15 is not replayable (the user's known-bad day); every session from 09-16 to 09-28 now replays exactly.

**Method notes.** The last boundary of a session is the close itself (23:30): the 23:15 to 23:30 bar completes after the market is shut. Live never processed it (the session ended first; the next morning's missed-flip reconcile handled it, 09-23 09:00). Hestia live will build that bar (`LiveData` polls five minutes past the close while the host stops at the close, so whether it arrives before `Stop` is a race), and Hestia has no post-close order refusal, so **the engine declines it by design**: a `BarComplete` or `ProvisionalBar` whose boundary is at or after the session close (derived as the rollover time plus 15 minutes, since `SessionStart` does not carry the close) sends no request and does not advance `last_processed_boundary`, so the next start's missed-flip reconcile still sees it. This was found in the replay: a first version stopped the replay at 23:29 and hid the fact that a bar injected from the next session's seed line (below) produced a 23:30 flip and entry on 09-22 that live never took. The replay now runs to 23:40, the guard does the work, and removing it breaks 09-22 (mutation-checked). The comparison also needed one data fix: the seed line `Last 15m flip:` in the next session's log carries the bar a previous session ended before reaching (09-22 23:15), so `recorded.py` parses it and `add_logged_contract` adds it when no session logged the bar. Bar-driven tolerance is 10 s because live start-up decisions land a few seconds after 09:00.

**Faults injected on recorded days** (`tests/test_prometheus_engine_replay.py`, the day's decisions must still all reproduce): an engine crash mid-trade (resumed from saved state, exactly as many orders as the clean run); the recorded kill and restart with a position open, on 09-21 and 09-22 (the engine comes back with its saved trade counter and takes live's later decisions); a rejected first exit attempt on a target (the retry goes out under `-lot1-2`, the first id never reaches the book); an UNCONFIRMED exit for 90 s (past the 30 s fill-wait, the engine is told UNCONFIRMED, waits, one order per target, ledger and engine agree at the end). The comparison is not permissive: moving the first target from 2.2% to 1.0% makes the 09-24 replay differ. Four days chained on one fake Hestia (09-22 to 09-25, each run past the close and stopped at 23:35) reproduce 43 decisions and end holding the position live held (trade 46, bearish, 5 units, ledger minus 10 lots).

**Ports of production's own tests** (`tests/test_prometheus_engine_ports.py`, 20, including the close guard): the missed-flip reconcile cases (baseline without a watermark, entry when the guards are clear, deferral and retry, drop when the state moved on, Rule 7 when in a trade, exit only inside the entry guard, agreeing flip, no flip, multi-day coalescing), the provisional margin guard for entries and flips in both directions and the warm-up skip, and sizing and the affordability check (static ignores margin, dynamic with a floor of one unit and a fallback when the margin read fails, the live margin used even with static sizing, no price means no entry). The other `tests/test_prometheus_*.py` files test the standalone process's host machinery (closing-time arithmetic, evening-only sessions, the Slack worker, stale pid, the session report, the DPL fetch) and have Hestia counterparts from P4; the engine's DPL behaviour is detect and alert only, as in production.

**Open points carried to P6.** (1) Hestia has no order refusal after the session close; the engine's own close guard covers bars, but a stop or target fired by LTP after 23:30 (a tick after the close) would still be sent. Worth a host-level refusal or a look at DRY_RUN cutover behaviour. (2) The provisional-bar path was replayed on the logged days (one to three provisional verdicts on most days in the window) with no decision difference. (3) The roll flows remain verified only by unit tests and the fake; the first real exercise is around 2026-10-12/13.
