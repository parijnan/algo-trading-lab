Decisions taken while building: separate close and open requests for a stop-and-reverse (no netted flip); the minute-bar net covers stops and targets only, a missed ENTRY touch is counted and never chased; a ledger mismatch at start drops the engine's trades and flattens what the ledger holds; one trade open at a time is asserted by the machine test. ## 1. What Oleus is

- **Config (the "plain thirds" the owner picked on 2026-10-21):** control levels (`--source previous_session`), touch entries, three equal lots with lot k exiting at target k, one stop for all open lots, stop scale 100% (the Camarilla stop level), no trailing stop, no re-buy, no counter trade, no entry limit per setup, no entry-hour filter (the 10, 11, 21 and 22 o'clock hours were tested on 2026-10-21 and are not a significant drag: permutation p = 0.55, and the worst hours picked on one half of the years earned money in the other half). Time rules: no entry in the first `AT_OPEN_MINUTES` and none in the last `ENTRY_CUTOFF_MIN` (60) minutes, everything open exits `HARD_EXIT_MIN` (15) minutes before the last bar. Flat overnight, so no positional rollover.
- **Backtest on that config (one lot per leg, Rs 10 per point per lot, gross, 903 sessions to 19 Oct 2026):** 2,404 trades (about 2.7 a day), +260.6k, max drawdown -48.0k, Calmar 5.43, about Rs 108 per trade or Rs 36 per leg, worst day -30.2k (11 Mar 2026), positive in every year (2023 +50.3k, 2024 +39.0k, 2025 +92.7k, 2026 +78.6k at three lots). Against Prometheus (live config, same window, same instrument): daily correlation 0.13, Prometheus earned almost everything in 2026.
- **Unit:** one unit is one full set of three lots (`lots_per_unit = 3`). Janus never held more than three lots at once in the backtest (one setup at a time).
- **Sizing:** the margin formula is the owner's, `LTP x lot size / 3 x multiplier`, with the multiplier 4 (risk-taking) or 6 (conservative: the same buffer per lot as Prometheus's two lots at 4). Which one is the owner's call (open decision D2). The three lots make it a per-unit figure, so set it up the way Prometheus's is (no separate lots factor) and pin it in a test.

## 2. Decisions the owner has to make (nothing below proceeds past step 1 without them)

- **D1. Replace Prometheus, or run beside it?** The owner's route (2026-10-22) is to run them in parallel by giving Oleus a different Angel One product type: INTRADAY for Oleus, CARRYFORWARD for Prometheus (as the other three engines). The broker keeps a separate position row per product type, so the two engines would no longer net against each other at the broker, and each engine's ledger would reconcile against its own row. That is the right shape, but Hestia does not support it today (code read 2026-10-22): the product type is one global setting (`AngelConfig.product = 'CARRYFORWARD'` in `hestia_core/angel_broker.py`, written into every order at `_place_chunk`), the position book is read into a dict keyed by token only (`_positions_blocking`; two rows for one token, one per product, would overwrite each other and the ledger would see the wrong net quantity), and the ghost-order search matches on symbol, side and quantity. The change is listed in step 1.1 and step 2. A fifth live engine still changes the margin pool arithmetic (`MarginSnapshot.sharing_engines`, total margin divided by the number of engines); running beside Prometheus makes it five, replacing Prometheus keeps it at four. Whether Prometheus is switched off later is a separate decision, no longer a blocker.
- **D2. The sizing multiplier, 4 or 6** (section 1).
- **D3. Costs are not in any number above.** At about Rs 36 per leg and roughly six order events per trade (three entries, up to three exits) the edge is thin. The cost study (step 0.2) is a gate, not a nice-to-have: if the edge does not survive a realistic round-trip cost per leg, the plan stops here.
- **D4. Execution style.** Hestia requests are market-style (`OpenRequest`, `CloseRequest`, `FlipRequest`, `FlattenRequest` carry no price). The backtest fills a touched entry at the level and a target at the target level (limit-type), and a touched stop at the stop level. Live, every one of those becomes a market order after the touch. The slippage between the two is the main live-versus-backtest risk (section 4). Proposal: build with market orders on touch, measure the shortfall in paper and in the first live weeks, and only then decide whether limit orders for targets are worth extending the interface (core, broker port, paper broker and reconciliation all touch it).

## 3. Steps

### Step 0. Research gates before any engine code (backtest side, no Delos, no broker)

- **0.1 Record what the owner has already decided.** Write the 2026-10-21 results (stop-scale grid, one-entry grid, hour-of-entry test, the Prometheus comparison and the replacement view at 2 and 3 lots) into `plans/janus-state-machine.md`, commit the four Janus files that are uncommitted (`janus_configs.py`, `janus_machine.py`, `machine_run.py`, `tests/test_janus_machine.py`; stop scale and entry limit are default-off so the plain config is unchanged), only when the owner says so.
- **0.2 Cost and slippage study.** Add to the backtest an adverse slippage per fill (ticks on entries, stops and targets separately; `TICK_THROUGH` and `COSTS_PCT` already exist as hooks, read what they do first) and a round-trip cost per leg (brokerage, exchange charges, CTT on the sell side, GST). Calibrate the slippage from real fills: Prometheus's live entries and exits are in `hestia_data/trades/prometheus_trades.csv` and the request journal (compare the fill with the touch price), and the average live entry gap against the backtest is already in the hestia-backtest comparison (`compare_actual.py` reports it per engine; on the 20 Oct 2026 run Selene paid 16.7 points worse on average). Gate: Calmar and total at the calibrated costs and 1, 2 and 3 ticks of slippage; the plan continues only if the edge per leg stays positive with room. Report the break-even slippage in ticks.
- **0.3 Contract choice.** Read `janus_data.load_sessions` and confirm which contract each session uses and how the previous session is defined across holidays, evening-only sessions and expiries (`MAX_PREV_SESSION_GAP_DAYS`, `MIN_BARS_PER_SESSION`). The engine must pick the same contract and compute the same levels; and Hestia refuses opens on a contract inside its tender window (`LIMIT_REFUSED`, roll window), so the backtest's contract in the last days before expiry has to be checked against that rule. Any session where they differ is either a backtest correction or an engine rule.
- **0.4 Live-tick sensitivity.** The backtest walks each one-minute bar on an assumed path (neutral; adverse is the sensitivity, `order_sensitive` marks trades that change). Live ticks resolve that order, so on the trades marked order-sensitive the engine will sometimes do better or worse than the neutral log. Quantify from the existing adverse-order run how much total and drawdown move, and use the adverse run, not the neutral one, as the planning number.

### Step 1. Read-only interface checks (code only)

- **1.1 Product type per engine (replaces the two-engines-on-one-contract question).** Read `hestia_core/ports.py` (`OrderSpec`, `PositionRow`), `angel_broker.py` (`_place_chunk`, `_positions_blocking`, `_find_ghost`, the order-update fill matching), `broker_router.py`, `paper_broker.py` and `core.py` (bootstrap against the broker book, the reconcile and the margin gate) and write down exactly what must carry the product type: a per-engine product on the registry entry (`EngineEntry`, default CARRYFORWARD so the four live engines are untouched), the product on every order, the position read keyed by token and product, the bootstrap and reconcile comparing an engine only with the rows of its own product, the ghost search including the product, and the paper broker ignoring it. Read 2026-10-22 08:23 IST (a one-off local login, terminated straight after, before Hestia's 08:55 start): all four open rows (CRUDEOILM, GOLDPETAL, NATGASMINI, SILVERMIC) come back with `producttype: CARRYFORWARD`, one row per token, quantities in contract units (`netqty`), so the field is present and is what Hestia's orders write today; no INTRADAY row existed, so separate rows per product for one token is still unconfirmed. Confirm from a real position-book response (read-only, via Hestia's own recorded responses or a one-off read on Delos with approval) that Angel returns separate rows per product type for one token, and that the product type of a fill is available from the order book for the fill matching. Also confirm three broker facts that decide the design: the INTRADAY margin Angel actually blocks for CRUDEOILM (it is usually lower than carry-forward, which changes the sizing multiplier discussion in D2), the time at which Angel auto-squares-off open INTRADAY positions on MCX (Oleus's hard exit is 15 minutes before the last bar; the broker's square-off must come after it, or the two collide and the broker's charges apply), and whether an unclosed INTRADAY position is auto-squared-off or converted. The last two are facts to read from Angel's documentation or support, not to assume.
- **1.2 Data the engine can see.** `DataSpec` takes a timeframe, a Supertrend period and a multiplier (Oleus has no Supertrend). Find out whether an engine can register a 1-minute `DataSpec` and receive a completed-minute bar (`BarComplete`), whether `ctx.latest_bar` returns the last one-minute bar, and how fresh `ctx.ltp` is. The engine needs ticks for touches (primary) and the completed one-minute high and low as a safety net for a touch that fell between two polls of the quote (the poll is 0.5 to 1 second; a brief spike can be missed, and a missed stop is the dangerous case). If a one-minute event does not exist, the smallest Hestia change that provides it is part of step 2.
- **1.3 Trade record.** `TRADE_RECORD_COLUMNS` is the 26-column Prometheus format with two lot columns. Oleus trades have three lots and about 2.7 trades a day. Decide how a three-lot trade is written (a third lot pair of columns, or the existing columns with a per-lot detail in the running rows), and check the Hestia Dashboard, the hestia-backtest ledger and the hestia-qc arithmetic against that format before they see a first Oleus row.
- **1.4 Sizing hooks.** `static_units`, the cap, the Slack sizing override and the dynamic rule: confirm Oleus can plug into them unchanged with `lots_per_unit = 3`.

### Step 2. Build `oleus_engine/` (nothing registered, nothing enabled)

Files, mirroring the other engines: `engine_configs.py` (every parameter, no magic numbers; the levels, setups, time rules and sizing live here), `levels.py` (the Camarilla levels from the previous session's high, low and close, and the margin per unit), `machine.py` (the setup state machine as a pure function: given the state and a price step it returns the events; this is a copy of the tick-level logic in `janus_machine.py`, not an import of research code, with a test that runs both on random walks and requires identical events, as the Janus machine already does against the older engine), `state.py` (the persisted state), `engine.py` (the `Engine` protocol: tick loop, requests, reconciliation, alerts), `replay_check.py`, `seed_state.py`.

Design points worth stating because they differ from the trend engines:

- **No Supertrend, no 15-minute boundary.** The engine is driven by price crossings of fixed levels and by the clock (entry cutoff, hard exit). Hestia's provisional-bar machinery is not used.
- **State.** Per setup (four of them): armed or fired for the first touch needed by the fade reclaim rule, whether the level was traded through, entries taken, open lots, booked lots, the stop, the three targets. Saved on every change through `ctx.save_state`, with the day's levels stored in the state so a restart rebuilds exactly the same machine (levels are fixed before the open and never change in a session). On a restart the ledger wins: if the broker holds lots that the saved state does not know about, adopt them as the open trade at the stored stop and targets, as the other engines do for an unexpected position, and alert.
- **Requests.** Entry: one `OpenRequest` of three lots. Each target: a `CloseRequest` of one lot. A stop: one `CloseRequest` (reason `stop_loss`, highest priority) for all remaining lots. A stop that opens the sister setup at the same level (a breakout's stop is the fade's entry level and the reverse): one netted `FlipRequest` when the sister's entry condition holds at that moment, otherwise a close and a later open. The hard exit and the session end: a `FlattenRequest`. Every request has a stable id built from the date, setup and event so a retry or a replay cannot double-send.
- **Touch detection.** Ticks first; the completed minute's high and low second, treated as a touch at the minute boundary and logged as a late catch, so the count of late catches in paper is itself a QC number.
- **Guards.** The same guards as the other engines where they make sense: stale price never triggers an entry (a stricter age than the trend engines because a touch is the whole signal), no entry on a stale quote, margin check before an entry, sizing admission.
- **Intraday and contract choice.** Flat before the close, no roll under an open position. Contract selection per step 0.3.
- **Excursions and logs.** Keep MFE and MAE per trade and the per-minute running rows (the backtest logs them; the dashboard and QC rely on the same figures).

Tests: unit tests for every transition and request, with mutation checks (each rule broken on purpose and caught, the method of `tests/test_janus_machine.py`), fake-Hestia scenarios for a gap, a partial fill, an UNCONFIRMED outcome, a restart mid-trade and a restart mid-flip, and the margin formula pinned.

### Step 3. Replay parity (the gate to deployment)

`oleus_engine/replay_check.py` drives the engine on the fake Hestia over real CRUDEOILM one-minute data, feeding each minute as the same assumed path the backtest walks (so the comparison is exact, not statistical), and compares every leg (setup, side, entry and exit time and price, reason) with `machine_run.py`'s output for the same sessions. Gate: every leg reproduced on a full recent window, fill prices equal to the cent, with each difference named and explained as the other replay checks do (an open trade at the window end, a session cut by the data). Then the same replay on the adverse bar order as the sensitivity.

### Step 4. Deploy in paper beside Prometheus

Register `ENGINES['oleus'] = EngineEntry(instrument='CRUDEOILM', factory='oleus_engine.engine:build', enabled=False, paper=True, lots_per_unit=3, product='INTRADAY')` (the `product` field is new, step 1.1) and enable it paper on Delos in `TRADING_HOSTS` (1 unit), after step 1.1 is settled. Paper does not exercise the product-type path at the broker, so the first live session at one unit is also the first real INTRADAY order path test: do it in a quiet session, with the broker position book read before and after. Add Oleus to the capitalised display names, the session report, the Slack sizing buttons, the dashboard config (`tracker_config.json`, `pull_snapshot.py`, the pin test), the hestia-backtest `ENGINES` registry and page arrays, and the hestia-qc and hestia-restart skill text. Deploy with the `hestia-restart` skill. Paper runs for at least two weeks of sessions; hestia-qc each of the first sessions, then weekly. What to measure in paper: trades and legs against the backtest of the same days (the hestia-backtest comparison logic, extended to a leg-by-leg match), slippage per fill against the touch price, late catches, UNCONFIRMED outcomes, restarts survived, and the cash and margin the paper engine used.

## 4. Risks, ordered by size

- **Costs and slippage (D3, D4).** The edge is Rs 36 per leg gross. Market orders on a touch will give some of it away on every entry, stop and target. Step 0.2 decides whether to go on.
- **A crisis day.** The -30.2k worst day (11 Mar 2026) is three stop-and-reverse chains on levels 4.5% of price wide, and it sets the drawdown. At live sizes it is an owner-level risk decision; candidates for a guard are a maximum stop width or a cap on stop-and-reverse chains (both listed as untried in the Janus plan), which would need their own research before they are built into the engine.
- **A missed touch.** A stop that is not seen is worse than an entry that is missed; the minute-bar safety net (step 1.2) is mandatory for stops.
- **Product type support in the shared core (D1).** The change touches the order path and the position read that all four live engines use. The default must leave CARRYFORWARD behaviour byte for byte unchanged, with a test that pins it, and the INTRADAY path is exercised in paper and replay first. A broker auto-square-off of INTRADAY positions is a second line of defence for Oleus (flat by the close) but also a source of unexpected fills if it comes before the hard exit.
- **Regime dependence.** Janus earned in every year of the sample but at different sizes (2025 best); one lot of Prometheus and one of Janus together had a daily correlation of 0.13. The sample is one instrument and three and a half years.
- **Order of events inside a minute.** The backtest's neutral path versus real ticks (step 0.4).
- **Interface growth.** If limit orders are ever added, that is a change to the core that all four live engines share and needs its own plan.
