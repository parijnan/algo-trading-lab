# MCX option hedging study: Tier C quote recorder (Fyers websocket)

Part of `plans/mcx-option-hedging.md` (Tier C, the forward record of spread and size). Fyers one-minute history has no bid, ask or sizes, so liquidity at the times a hedge would be bought and sold can only be measured by recording it forward. `quote_recorder.py` does that from the Fyers data websocket. Built 2026-10-30. A first version polled the REST quotes and depth endpoints; it was dropped the same day because Hestia's candle source runs Fyers first and shares the REST request budget (Standard plan: 5 a second, 50 a minute, 5,000 a day for all data requests).

## What it records

For CRUDEOIL, CRUDEOILM, NATURALGAS, NATGASMINI, SILVERM and GOLDM: the two nearest unexpired option expiries, every strike within 3% of the future each option is struck against, calls and puts, plus those futures (about 12), at most 96 symbols in all (the Standard plan allows 100 websocket symbols per user across connections). The allowance is shared equally between the (underlying, expiry) pairs, closest to the money first, and a pair that cannot use its share gives the rest to the others. Three windows a day (IST): the first 15 minutes of the session, the next 30, and 22:45 to the close. Windows follow the MCX calendar (`hestia_core.mcx_market.MarketCalendar`): 23:30 or 23:55 close, evening-only days start at 17:00, closed days record nothing.

Mode: SymbolUpdate (not lite). Each message is one symbol: bid and ask price and size (top of book only), last price, last traded quantity, volume traded today, total buy and sell quantity, circuit limits, exchange time. Near-the-money options update about every 1.8 s and the futures about every 0.9 s. The sockets carry no open interest; the symbol master's previous-day open interest is written beside each symbol in `universe_<window>.csv`. Five-level depth (DepthUpdate mode) is not recorded: it would add symbols against the 100 limit, and the top of book already answers the spread question.

## What is written, and when

Every received message is checked against the last one written for that symbol; a row goes to `ticks.csv` when the bid, ask, either size, the last price or the volume changed (about 85% of messages in the live check), and a quiet symbol is still written once every 60 seconds with `heartbeat=1`, so a quiet quote can be told from a silent feed. Total buy and sell quantity alone do not count as a change. Messages are drained and written once a second. Expect roughly 30 rows a second while recording, about 30 MB of CSV a day. Output (gitignored, `data_pipeline/data/mcx_option_quotes/<date>/`): `ticks.csv`, `universe_<window>.csv`, `summary_<window>.csv` (per symbol: messages received and rows written, share of rows with both sides quoted, median spread in rupees and as a percentage of mid), and in `logs/`: `option_recorder_<date>.log`, plus the Fyers SDK's own `logs/fyers_sdk/fyersDataSocket.log` (checked 30 Oct: it does not contain the token).

## Why it cannot disturb the live engines

It is a separate process that imports only the pure MCX calendar from Hestia, reads `hestia_data/fyers_token.json` and nothing else of Hestia's, writes only under its own output and log folders, never places an order and never calls Angel One. It makes no REST data request of its own. The Fyers SDK makes one POST to `/data/symbol-token` per `subscribe()` (to resolve symbols) with no timeout, so the recorder subscribes exactly twice per connection (the futures, then the options once they are priced) and ends a window by closing the connection, not by unsubscribing (which would be another POST): about 6 such calls a day. The SDK is a singleton per process and its dependency pins are not used: it is installed with `pip install --no-deps fyers-apiv3 "aws_lambda_powertools==1.25.5"` so nothing else in the environment changes.

The recorder idles, with no connection, while the token is not today's (mode 600, modified and issued today, not expired), while its own kill flag `option_recorder_off.flag` exists in its output folder (also checked every second inside a window), and outside its windows. A refused token ends the window without reconnecting. A feed silent for 30 s is logged; silent for 90 s the connection is closed and opened again, at most 3 times a window. The SDK's own reconnect (5 tries) re-subscribes everything in one call.

## Running it

`python research/option_hedge/quote_recorder.py --plan` prints today's windows. Started once a day on weekdays before the first window, it sleeps between windows and exits after the last. Suggested cron (Delos, not installed; Delos needs `fyers-apiv3` installed first): `50 8 * * 1-5 cd /home/parijnan/scripts/algo-trading-lab && nice -n 10 /home/parijnan/anaconda3/bin/python research/option_hedge/quote_recorder.py >> logs/option_recorder_cron_$(date +\%Y\%m\%d).out 2>&1`. Tests: `python -m pytest tests/test_option_hedge_recorder.py` (24, with a fake clock and a fake socket; the guards were broken on purpose and caught).

## What the first live checks showed (30 Oct, about 10:00 IST)

Two back-to-back 100-second windows with the real SDK and the full universe (84 options and 12 futures): all 96 symbols sent data, about 3,800 messages a window, close and connect again worked. November 9850 call: median spread 0.93 (0.19% of mid), 54 messages in 100 s. December 9700 call: bid and ask 212 apart (28% of mid), a quote that is not tradable. So the front expiry is liquid near the money and the second one may not be, which the recording will quantify.
