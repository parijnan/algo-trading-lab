How each trade was sized

At every entry: units = round down of (account value ÷ 4 ÷ the engine's margin per unit at the entry price), capped at 100. The account value is Rs 4,00,000 plus the profit of every trade that had already closed. If that is below one unit, the entry is skipped. A trade keeps its units until it closes. The divisor is 4 because all four engines are live.

Charges on every order, for non-agricultural commodity futures: brokerage Rs 20; exchange transaction fee 0.0021% of the order value; SEBI fee Rs 10 per crore; GST 18% on those three; stamp duty 0.002% on buy orders; commodity transaction tax (CTT) 0.01% on sell orders. Order value is price × contract size × lots × units, using the entry price for both orders of a trade. Each trade has an entry order and an exit order; a Prometheus trade that books lot 1 at its first target and lot 2 later has a second exit order, and a Janus trade has one exit order for each different exit among its three lots. A reversal that the live engine sends as one netted request is counted as two orders. The rates are the ones brokers publish for MCX futures; check them against a contract note.

Janus has no live trades yet, so its slippage is assumed to match Prometheus per lot: Prometheus's measured shortfall divided by its two lots, times Janus's three lots.

Slippage is the average shortfall of the live trades against their backtest twins (entry and exit together), per unit per trade, then multiplied by the units taken. It comes from the live trades that matched a backtest trade with the same exit. It is applied only where it is a cost: Helios measures slightly better than its backtest, so it is set to nil. Costs and slippage come off a trade's profit when it closes, so the account value that sizes later entries is already net of them.

What to keep in mind

These are backtest trades, not live fills. The live book has weeks of history; the Hestia Integrated Backtest page shows how closely they agree.
Costs depend heavily on the starting capital. At Rs 4,00,000 every engine trades one to three units for years, where a fixed Rs 20 brokerage and the percentage charges take a large share of each trade's profit. The slippage figures come from a few weeks of live trades, 12 to 28 per engine, so they are rough: Prometheus's average is driven by one stop that filled much worse than its backtest.
Most of the gain comes late: see the share of profit by year. Compounding on a larger account turns the same edge into much bigger rupee wins in the last year.
The account value moves only when a trade closes. Open-trade losses, and the daily mark-to-market cash movement on futures, are not in it, so the drops shown are closed-trade drops.
Today's margin formulas are applied to 2023 prices. Helios and Typhon use a single observed margin quote scaled by 4; the exchange margin has changed over time.
Rolls are separate trades: when a position is rolled, the reopened trade is sized afresh from the account value at that moment.
